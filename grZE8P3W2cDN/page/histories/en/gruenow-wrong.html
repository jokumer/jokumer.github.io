History

Why exactly “Verkehrt-Grünow”?

Until the early 20th century, the Uckermark village in the lowlands of the Welse river bore the unofficial name “Verkehrt-Grünow” to distinguish it from the place of the same name near Prenzlau. The name derives from the unusual construction of the medieval fieldstone church, whose tower stands not in the west, but above the choir in the east.

According to a legend, this peculiarity came about through a dispute between two noble sisters who, in an ambitious rivalry, each wanted to have their own church tower built for the house of worship. Each of the sisters had a tower built on a different side of the church to demonstrate her influence and importance in the village. As the conflict escalated and neither was willing to give in, the situation was finally resolved by the death of one of the sisters. Thereupon the surviving sister had the tower built by the deceased on the west side demolished, so that only the eastern tower remained and the church received its unusual appearance.

The church is considered the only choir-tower church in the Uckermark and was built around 1250 from carefully hewn fieldstones. It has a short nave, a recessed choir with apse and a northern sacristy.
